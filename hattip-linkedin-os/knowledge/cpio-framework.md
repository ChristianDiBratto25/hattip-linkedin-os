# Hat Tip CPIO Writing Framework

Use this framework before you write any LinkedIn post.

CPIO stands for Convey, Package, Information, and Order.

Complete the four steps in sequence. Do not draft the post until each step is clear.

All writing must use simple English that follows ASD-STE100 principles where practical. Use short, direct sentences. Choose common words. Remove filler. Make each sentence easy to understand on the first read.

The final post must also follow all Hat Tip prohibited writing rules.

## C: CONVEY

First, define what the post must communicate.

Write one clear sentence that states the purpose of the post.

The purpose can be a story, opinion, hot take, framework, lesson, process, observation, case study, company update, or another clear idea supported by the source material.

The post should have one main purpose.

Do not combine several unrelated ideas into one post.

Ask these questions before moving forward:

What should the reader understand after reading this post?

What part of the source material supports that idea?

What result should the post create for the author?

The last question must have a literal answer. Examples include building trust, showing expertise, sharing a founder story, explaining a process, or creating discussion around a real opinion.

If you cannot state the purpose in one clear sentence, stop. Review the source material again.

Do not invent a stronger opinion, lesson, story, or conclusion than the source supports.

CPIO OUTPUT:

Convey: [Write one sentence that states the exact purpose of the post.]

## P: PACKAGE

Next, decide how to present the idea on LinkedIn.

Packaging controls the angle, format, hook posture, and structure used to present the idea.

The package must fit the source material.

Do not force a format because it is familiar or easy to write.

Consider the available Hat Tip methods and any current project instructions.

Possible packages can include a founder story, bold opinion, process breakdown, framework, case study, borrowed authority post, news reaction, tactical breakdown, industry prediction, or another approved method.

Choose the package that gives the source idea the strongest clear presentation.

The package should create interest without changing the meaning of the source.

The hook must leave useful information unresolved so the reader has a reason to continue.

Do not reveal the full insight in the hook.

Do not create false suspense.

Do not use unsupported numbers, timeframes, results, or claims to make the package stronger.

Do not use a formulaic structure when a more natural structure fits the source better.

Before moving forward, confirm that the package fits the author, the idea, and the available evidence.

CPIO OUTPUT:

Package: [State the chosen method or format.]

Angle: [Explain in one or two sentences how the idea will be presented.]

Hook posture: [Describe what information the hook will lead with and what useful information will remain unresolved.]

## I: INFORMATION

Now decide which information the post needs.

Use only information that supports the Convey and Package decisions.

Pull the required information from the transcript, the author's files, project context, approved research, or other verified sources.

Do not add information because it sounds useful.

Do not include every detail from the source.

Choose the facts, examples, stories, explanations, quotes, and context needed to make the post complete.

Every part of the post must serve a purpose.

A post can be long when the idea needs detail.

Do not make a post longer than the idea requires.

Remove any sentence that does not improve the argument, story, clarity, evidence, or reader value.

Aim for high information density. Communicate the full idea in the fewest words that preserve its meaning and value.

Verify every specific claim.

Never fabricate statistics, numbers, percentages, quantities, dates, timeframes, revenue figures, performance metrics, or other factual details.

If the source gives an approximate figure, keep it approximate.

If required information is missing, do not guess. Flag the missing information.

CPIO OUTPUT:

Required information: [List the specific source-supported facts, stories, examples, and context required for the post.]

Exclude: [State any source material that is related but should not appear because it would weaken focus.]

Missing information: [State any information required to write the post accurately. Write “None” when complete.]

## O: ORDER

Finally, decide how the information should flow.

Arrange the information so the post is easy to follow and supports the chosen package.

Each section should lead naturally into the next.

The hook should create enough interest to continue.

The next section should give the reader enough context to understand the topic.

The body should develop the idea in the order that makes it easiest to understand.

Evidence, examples, and detail should appear where they strengthen the point being made.

The ending should complete the idea naturally.

Do not add a forced summary.

Do not repeat the same conclusion in different words.

Do not add a CTA unless the source, strategy, or brief calls for one.

CPIO OUTPUT:

Order:

Hook: [What the opening must establish.]

Setup: [What context comes next.]

Development: [How the main idea will develop.]

Support: [Where examples, evidence, or details belong.]

Ending: [How the post should finish.]

## REQUIRED WRITING RULES

After CPIO is complete, write the post using the approved plan.

Use clear and simple language.

Prefer common words over complex words.

Use active voice when practical.

Keep sentences focused on one main idea.

Keep paragraphs easy to read on mobile.

Read the draft for natural flow.

Remove unnecessary words.

Preserve the author’s actual voice, opinions, experience, and level of certainty.

Do not make the author sound more certain, emotional, controversial, or successful than the source supports.

Do not invent transitions between facts when the source does not support the connection.

## PROHIBITED PHRASES

Never use “Here’s the thing.”

Never use “The best part?”

Never use “The secret?”

Never use “But here’s.”

Never use “What if I told you...”

Never use “In today’s fast-paced world...”

Never use “Gone are the days...”

Never use “Let me explain...”

Never use “Here’s why...”

Never use “Let me tell you...”

Never use the word “shift.”

## PROHIBITED STRUCTURES

Do not use reversal framing.

This includes structures such as “It’s not X, it’s Y,” “This isn’t A. It’s B,” “X isn’t the problem. Y is,” or any similar contrast.

Do not introduce a common belief only to reverse it.

Do not use structures such as “Most people think X. The truth is Y,” “Everyone focuses on A. The real leverage is B,” or similar constructions.

Do not use rhetorical questions.

Every question in a post must expect a literal answer.

Do not use repeated sentence openings to create artificial rhythm.

Avoid patterns such as “More X. More Y. More Z,” “Your X. Your Y. Your Z,” or similar repetition.

Do not use stacked sentence fragments.

Avoid sequences such as “Strategy. Taste. Judgment.”

Write complete, connected sentences instead.

Never start a sentence with “Most” when making a broad claim about people, founders, brands, companies, or another group.

Do not use abstract comparisons such as “The math compounds in ways acquisition can’t.”

State the concrete effect supported by the source.

## PROHIBITED WRITING PATTERNS

Do not use corporate buzzwords.

Do not use cliché openings.

Do not use generic AI phrasing.

Do not use unnecessary adverbs or filler words.

Do not create artificial symmetry between sentences.

Do not use dramatic cadence for effect.

Do not over-explain background information.

Do not use em dashes unless they are required by source text. Prefer a period, comma, or parentheses.

Do not use bold or underlined text.

Do not add artificial summaries or conclusions.

## HOOK RULES

The hook must create genuine interest in the source idea.

It should create a clear reason to continue reading without giving away the full insight.

Do not use rhetorical questions.

Do not use reversal framing.

Do not begin with “Most people,” “The truth is,” or “The reality.”

Do not use generic formulas such as “X reasons...” or “Here’s what I learned...” by default.

Do not use a round number when the source provides a more specific verified figure.

Do not invent a specific figure because it would improve the hook.

Do not use vague claims that could apply to many people or companies.

Do not create artificial suspense.

The hook must be supported by the post that follows.

## FINAL CPIO CHECK

Before drafting, confirm that Convey states one clear purpose.

Confirm that Package is the strongest source-supported way to present that purpose.

Confirm that Information contains only the material needed to execute the post.

Confirm that Order gives that information a clear and natural flow.

If any section is weak or unclear, improve the CPIO plan before drafting.

After drafting, compare the finished post with the CPIO plan.

The post should achieve the original Convey goal.

The package should remain consistent.

Every important claim must trace to source material.

Every sentence should earn its place.

The final post must be free of all prohibited phrases, structures, and writing patterns.
